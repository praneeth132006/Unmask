What happened

An email with the subject “Bevestig je deelname & win een Efteling-cadeaukaart 🎢✨” arrived from “Efteling Bezoekerspanel” <Efteling-Bezoekerspanel[@]𝗲𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴-client-58853181[.]𝗻𝗹>. It was examined entirely on the analyst's computer: its sender details, its links, its attachments and the way it was delivered.

It looks like password phishing (medium confidence): It wants you to sign in on a fake page or type your password.

The main reasons

  • A link says one website but really goes to another.
  • If you reply, your answer goes to no-reply@team-efteling.nl, not to the address it came from.

Why it matters to the organisation

RiskWhat could happenLevel
Stolen passwordsSomeone who enters a password on the fake page hands attackers the account. From there they can read mail and files, send more phishing from a trusted address and reach other systems.High
PrivacyThe email contains 41 hidden tracking pixels. When the email is opened, the sender learns it was read and roughly where.Low

What to do now

Everyone

  • Do not click links, open attachments, scan QR codes or reply to this email.
  • Do not forward it to colleagues. Report it using the steps below, then delete it.
  • If you are unsure whether it is genuine, contact the supposed sender using a phone number or website you already know, never the details in the email.

If someone already clicked, opened a file, replied or typed a password

  • Tell the IT or security team immediately. Speed matters more than embarrassment.
  • Change the password for that account, and for any other account that uses the same password, from a different device.
  • If a file was opened, disconnect the computer from the network and wait for IT before using it again.
  • If money was sent or bank details were shared, call the bank at once and ask for the payment to be recalled.

For the IT and security team

  • Block the sender, the domains and the links listed in the technical part, and search every mailbox for the same email.
  • Remove copies from all mailboxes that received it.
  • For anyone who interacted with it: reset passwords, sign them out everywhere, review app approvals and check for new mailbox rules or forwarding.

Who to tell

  • Your internal IT or security team (first, always).
  • Anti-Phishing Working Group (APWG): reportphishing@apwg.org
  • Netcraft: scam@netcraft.com, for takedown of the fake sites
  • Google Safe Browsing: https://safebrowsing.google.com/safebrowsing/report_phish/
  • United States: https://reportfraud.ftc.gov and https://www.ic3.gov
  • United Kingdom: report@phishing.gov.uk (NCSC)
  • India: https://cybercrime.gov.in and incident@cert-in.org.in (CERT-In)
  • Elsewhere: your national CERT or cybercrime reporting centre. Serious losses should also be reported to the police and the bank.
