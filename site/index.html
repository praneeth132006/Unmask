How it protects you

Four moments where a scam usually wins.

01

It stops the click.

The email shows paypal.com. The link really goes to paypa1-secure.top. Unmask catches it before the page opens, and says why.

With a free urlscan.io key, you can even preview the page safely, in a sandbox, not in your browser.

02

It warns you inside your inbox.

Risky emails get a banner with the main reasons. Risky senders get a badge in the list, before you open anything.

03

It lets you peek without opening.

Opening an email loads its tracking pixels and tells the sender you read it. Peek checks it from the inbox and shows a text-only preview.

What it checks

Everything a phishing email tries to hide.

Who really sent it

SPF, DKIM and DMARC, verified locally. Look-alike domains like paypa1.com, display names hiding another address, replayed genuine emails.

Where links really go

Text that shows one site and links to another, redirect chains, QR codes in images and PDFs, and fake sign-in pages behind a link.

What attachments hide

Fake PDFs like Invoice.pdf.html, Office macros, programs in ZIPs, links inside PDFs and SVG "pictures" that run code.

Who's watching you read

Tracking pixels and click-tracked links from about 40 services, shown exactly where they sit, with images blocked.
