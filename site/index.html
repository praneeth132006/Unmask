See what a suspicious email is really hiding.

Unmask is a free Chrome extension that checks the email you open in Gmail: the real sender, where every link really goes, hidden text, trackers and risky attachments. It explains everything in plain language.

⬇ Download for Chrome Read the guide

Free and open source (MIT) · Works in Chrome, Edge and Brave · Your email never leaves your browser

Install in two minutes

Unmask isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet, so you load it yourself. No programming needed.

Download

From the latest release, download unmask-chrome-<version>.zip under Assets and unzip it. Keep the folder somewhere permanent.

Load it

Open chrome://extensions, switch on Developer mode, click Load unpacked and choose the unzipped folder (the one containing manifest.json).

Check an email

Pin Unmask, reload Gmail, open any email and click the Unmask icon. The report appears in a side panel.

Everything a phishing email tries to hide

Every check runs inside your browser. Online checks are optional and only run when you click them.

Sender

Who really sent it

SPF, DKIM and DMARC, display names hiding another address, and look-alike domains like paypa1.com for 50+ brands.

Links

Where links really go

Text that shows one site but links to another, redirect wrappers removed, and one-click redirect tracing.

Hover

Point at the problem

Hover a red flag and Unmask outlines the exact link, sender, attachment or phrase in the Gmail email.

Files

Risky attachments

Executables, fake PDFs (Invoice.pdf.html), macro documents, encrypted ZIPs, plus a VirusTotal check.

Body

Hidden text, safely shown

Invisible text used to fool spam filters, forms asking for passwords, and pressure phrases, in a safe view with scripts off.

Privacy

Trackers

Tracking pixels and click-tracked links from about 40 services, with images blocked so senders can't tell you opened it.

Hover a red flag, see it in the email

No more guessing which link or sentence a warning is about.

A closer look

Private by design

Stays on your device

The email is analyzed in your browser and never sent to an Unmask server. There are no accounts and no analytics.

Online checks are opt-in

VirusTotal and urlscan.io run only when you click them, and get a link or a file's fingerprint, not the email.

Free, and never billed

Free keys can't be charged; going over a limit just pauses that check. Limits and costs.

Unmask never says an email is "safe". A clean result means the checks found nothing, not that nothing is there. Stay careful with links and attachments you didn't expect.
